# Scalar Communication via Random Direction Refreshing for Distributed Optimization 

Mohammadreza Rostami and Solmaz S. Kia, Senior member, IEEE 

Abstract — Distributed optimization over networks requires agents to repeatedly exchange decision variables with their neighbors. When the decision dimension d is large, these exchanges dominate the communication cost, which is critical for bandwidth-constrained agents. Existing remedies quantize or sparsify the exchanged vectors, yet each message still scales with d and the compression error must be compensated by additional states. To address this limitation, we propose a scalar-communication mechanism in which every neighbor message carries a single real number regardless of d. Agents regenerate a common random direction from a shared seed, transmit only the inner product of their state with that direction, and act on the resulting rank-one surrogate of their neighbors’ states while retaining full local gradients. We develop and analyze the mechanism for an existing continuous-time distributed optimization algorithm. For strongly convex local costs with Lipschitz gradients, we show that the optimizer remains the unique consensus equilibrium, that a fixed direction admits spurious equilibria, and that refreshing the direction at a suf-ficiently high rate yields exponential mean-square and almost-sure convergence with constant gains and no residual error. The framework admits any isotropic fixed-norm direction distri-bution, including Rademacher, scaled-coordinate, and sphere-normalized Gaussian directions; all three attain lower fresh-encoding variance than unnormalized Gaussian directions. The effects of the direction distribution and the refresh interval are illustrated in simulations.

Related Work: In bandwidth-limited settings, the central concern is the size of each transmitted message, and two major approaches have been developed to address it. The first reduces the number of bits used to represent each entry of the exchanged vector. Building on quantized consensus [12], [13], [14], the effect of quantization on distributed subgradi-ent methods was first characterized in [15]. Subsequent work established data-rate requirements for convergence [16], de-signed quantizers tailored to optimization dynamics [17], and analyzed convergence under limited communication [18]. With a fixed quantizer, these methods generally converge to a neighborhood of the optimizer, whereas adaptive quanti-zation that progressively shrinks the quantization range can recover exact convergence [19], [20], achieving linear rates for smooth strongly convex objectives [21]. However, the communicated object remains a d-dimensional vector, so the message length still scales with the problem dimension. The second approach reduces the number of transmitted entries. In distributed learning, QSGD showed that aggressive quanti-zation trades communication against estimator variance [22], which motivated sparsification methods that retain only a subset of entries and use error memory to preserve discarded information [23], [24]. Koloskova et al. [25] extend this idea arXiv:2610.05666v1 [cs.MA] 5 Oct 2026 2

to decentralized networks, where agents maintain auxiliary estimates of their neighbors’ states to accommodate biased and unbiased compressors. Compressed gradient-tracking and push–pull methods add tracking variables to achieve linear convergence on directed networks and nonconvex objectives [26], [27]. The closest schemes to our approach are random sparsification operators that retain k of the d

entries [23], [25]. With k = 1 , the rescaled operator d·rand 1

is equivalent to the scaled coordinate-direction encoder con-sidered in this paper. In these methods, however, the selected indices are transmitted together with the retained values [23], and the resulting compression error is compensated using ei-ther error memory [23] or auxiliary neighbor estimates [25]. In summary, existing communication-efficient methods either exchange messages whose length scales with the problem dimension or rely on memory, tracking, or auxiliary states to compensate for compression error. In contrast, our scheme regenerates the communication direction, and hence any se-lected index, from a shared seed, so each message consists of a single real number and no compensating state is required. 

Contributions: This paper introduces a scalar-communication mechanism for distributed optimization in which neighboring agents exchange a single real number per message, regardless of the decision dimension. The mechanism is developed for the continuous-time coordination algorithm of [8], al-though it is applicable to any distributed optimization scheme whose inter-agent coupling depends only on relative states. By generating common random directions from a shared seed, agents communicate only scalar projections of their states, eliminating the need to transmit direction vectors or maintain compression-error memory. We show that the optimal consensus equilibrium of the original algorithm is preserved under scalar communication and remains the unique equilibrium on the zero-sum integral-state subspace. For strongly convex local objectives with globally Lipschitz gradients, we establish exponential mean-square and almost-sure convergence to the optimizer using constant gains, provided the direction-refresh interval is sufficiently small. The resulting refresh condition explicitly reveals how graph connectivity, objective regularity, and direction statistics in-fluence convergence. A key challenge is that the communica-tion direction is held fixed between refreshes. Although the resulting encoder is unbiased at each refresh instant, the held direction becomes coupled with the evolving network state over time. We address this challenge through a Lyapunov analysis that combines conditional drift at refresh times with bounds on the evolution between refreshes. Finally, we characterize the fresh-encoding variance of unnormal-ized Gaussian, Rademacher, scaled-coordinate, and spherical directions. The convergence theorem applies to the latter three fixed-norm distributions; scaled-coordinate directions recover random-coordinate communication as a special case. Compared to our prior work on scalar communication for federated learning [9], [10], this paper extends the framework from client-server aggregation to distributed neighbor-to-neighbor interactions, where the communicated quantities are dynamically coupled through the network. The continuous-time re-sults demonstrate convergence with scalar-valued exchange, providing the basis for such bandwidth-constrained imple-mentations. V. C ONCLUSIONS 

We introduced a scalar-communication mechanism for consensus-based distributed optimization in which each 7

neighbor message carries a single real number regardless of the decision dimension. Agents regenerate a common random direction from a shared seed, transmit the projection of their state onto that direction, and act on a rank-one surrogate of their neighbors’ states. We showed that the optimal consensus equilibrium is preserved, that fixed direc-tions can introduce spurious equilibria, and that sufficiently frequent direction refreshes yield exponential mean-square and almost-sure convergence with constant gains and no residual error for strongly convex objectives with Lipschitz gradients. Simulations illustrated the effects of the direction distribution and refresh interval. Future work includes a discrete-time formulation with sampled and quantized scalar messages, tighter refresh-interval bounds, extensions to di-rected and time-varying networks, robustness to unreliable communication channels through redundant scalar transmis-sions, and the combination of scalar communication with event-triggered scheduling to reduce both message size and communication frequency.
