# GQ-FSL: Green Quantized Federated Split Learning 

Idan Roth and Lutz Lampe 

The University of British Columbia, Vancouver, BC, Canada, {idanroth, Lampe}@ece.ubc.ca 

Abstract —Deploying state-of-the-art deep neural networks (DNNs) at the wireless edge is severely bottlenecked by the strict energy and resource constraints of mobile devices. While federated split learning (FSL) mitigates on-device computation by offloading workloads to an edge server, this may introduce sys-temic overheads, while the continuous exchange of cut-layer data, and submodels still incurs significant energy consumption (EC). To address this, we propose a green quantized FSL (GQ-FSL) framework that incorporates stochastic quantization for both local collaborative training and wireless transmissions. Notably, GQ-FSL supports asymmetric precision levels for the client-and server-side submodels, effectively decoupling device energy constraints from global convergence degradation. To quantify these tradeoffs, we develop parameterized energy models for the split architecture and derive a theoretical convergence bound under statistically heterogeneous data. Building on that, we formulate a joint optimization problem to configure the DNN split point and precision levels, minimizing the total system EC while satisfying a strict target accuracy constraint. Ultimately, we demonstrate that GQ-FSL enables large-scale DNN deploy-ment on resource-constrained devices, achieving superior energy efficiency compared to quantized federated learning and full-precision FSL. 

Index Terms —Federated split learning, quantized neural net-works, energy efficiency, wireless edge networks. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Energy and resource limitations on mobile devices create a bottleneck for deploying state-of-the-art deep neural net-works (DNNs) at the network edge [1]. Although federated learning (FL) enables privacy-preserving distributed training [2], [3], its requirement for full on-device training renders standard FL impractical for resource-constrained devices [4], [5]. Split learning (SL)-based frameworks, notably federated split learning (FSL) [6], alleviate this burden by partitioning the DNN and offloading the primary computational workload to an edge server [5]. However, aggregating these workloads can overwhelm resource-limited edge servers, and the contin-uous exchange of large intermediate activations and gradients, alongside submodels, can substantially increase energy con-sumption (EC) [7], [8]. Recent energy-efficient FSL frame-works explored system-level optimizations, including device-specific split point adaptation to balance energy and latency [9], dynamic device clustering to address data heterogeneity [10], and joint power allocation, client scheduling, and split-layer selection for heterogeneous edge networks [11]. Model compression, particularly quantization, provides a direct means to reduce these overheads. By lowering the nu-merical precision of parameters and activations [12], quantiza-tion reduces computational complexity, memory requirements, and communication payloads, thereby substantially lowering EC [1], [4]. Although recent works apply quantization to improve the energy efficiency of standard FL [4], [13], these approaches still require full on-device training and thus remain impractical for highly resource-constrained edge devices. Inte-grating quantization into FSL architectures offers a promising path to minimize the total energy footprint, yet the complex interactions among quantization precision, split architecture, and EC remain largely unexplored. Motivated by these open challenges, this paper investigates a quantized neural network (QNN)-based FSL framework de-signed for energy-efficient collaborative training on resource-constrained devices. Our primary contributions are as follows:  

> •

We introduce green quantized FSL (GQ-FSL), a framework that integrates stochastic quantization into local training and wireless transmission processes. By permitting distinct pre-cision levels for client and server QNNs, GQ-FSL reduces computations, memory accesses, and communication EC.  

> •

To quantify these savings, we derive split-based parameter-ized energy models for QNN-based local training using a generic DNN processing chip, as well as for transmissions.  

> •

We derive a rigorous convergence bound that characterizes the impact of stochastic quantization in the presence of sta-tistically heterogeneous data, thereby formalizing the funda-mental trade-off between energy savings and quantization-induced convergence degradation. This work refines and extends prior QNN-based FL analysis [4].  

> •

Building on these derivations, we jointly optimize precision levels and the split point to minimize total EC subject to a target-accuracy constraint. We demonstrate that GQ-FSL not only facilitates large-scale DNN deployment on resource-constrained devices but also achieves superior en-ergy efficiency compared to standard quantized FL. II. The results confirm that GQ-FSL maintains strict energy superiority over both benchmarks at every evaluated accuracy level. This is attributed to the fact that FSL allows for burden sharing and collaborative training with a powerful edge server. Specifically, the dominant off-chip memory ac-cesses dramatically decrease, along with the communication-expensive model exchange for aggregation, which is done based on a lightweight submodel. As expected, total EC naturally increases as the target accuracy becomes more strict. A notable rise in EC occurs when tightening the threshold to ϵ = 0.05 . At this extreme precision requirement, the allowable quantization noise floor forces all frameworks to rely heavily on extended global rounds and high precision levels, which drastically amplifies the total EC. Even under these severe constraints, GQ-FSL successfully leverages its dynamic architecture and decoupled precision to mitigate the energy spike, outperforming the baselines. Table I summarizes the optimal system parameters, min-imum total EC ( Emin ), and required global rounds ( Tmin )for a target accuracy of ϵ = 0 .1 across selected K values. Once again, GQ-FSL consistently achieves the lowest total EC across all settings, significantly outperforming Q-FL and FP-FSL. Notably, the absolute minimum energy is achieved at K = 1 , effectively collapsing the parallel FSL framework into sequential SL. While seemingly counterintuitive since more participating clients accelerate convergence, this result perfectly aligns with the objective function in Eq. (14), which monotonically increases with K. Specifically, its right-hand side is linear in K, and the expected server broadcasting energy also increases with K, as transmission rate is deter-mined by the worst link of the K clients. Although Tmin 

decreases as K grows, it reaches a limit of diminishing returns, plateauing towards 278 rounds for K > 10 and failing to offset the increasing per-round energy cost. To manage this energy-convergence tradeoff, GQ-FSL exploits its decoupled precision. The optimal split point predominantly settles at 

ℓc = 4, strategically offloading the bulk of the DNN to the edge server ( 28 × more weights). Absorbing this large computational load, its precision ( qs) remains rigidly anchored at 19 bits, balancing between minimizing its substantial EC and preventing quantization bias from breaching the target ac-curacy. Conversely, the client precision ( qc) exhibits a general downward trend from 21 to 18 bits as K increases, actively reducing critical computation and communication EC when 

Tmin decreases. Finally, the shift to ℓc = 5 at K = 10 proves that architectural flexibility outweighs rigid precision scaling. Rather than adopting a more costly qs = 20 to lower Tmin like Q-FL, GQ-FSL strategically redistributes the computational load, maintaining qs = 19 for optimal energy efficiency. V. C ONCLUSION 

This paper proposed the GQ-FSL framework to advance green edge AI and overcome the resource bottlenecks of deploying large-scale DNNs at the wireless edge. By inte-grating FSL and stochastic quantization with distinct precision levels on server and client sides, our framework successfully decouples strict client resource constraints from global model accuracy. We formulated a joint optimization problem rooted in a theoretical convergence bound to strategically configure both the network split point and precision levels. Simulations validate our theoretical analysis and confirm that GQ-FSL is effective in minimizing total system EC, outperforming quantized FL and standard FSL. Ultimately, this approach pro-vides a practical framework for sustainable edge intelligence while simultaneously enabling resource-constrained devices to participate in complex collaborative training.
